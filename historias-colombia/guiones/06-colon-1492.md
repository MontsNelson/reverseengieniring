# Episode 6: Colón y el 12 de octubre de 1492

**Length:** about 56 s (7 clips × 8 s)
**Hook text (0–2 s):** "¿Por qué Colombia se llama COLOMBIA? 🇨🇴🚢" ("Why is Colombia called COLOMBIA?")
**Nico's action:** he stows away on the Santa María, gets seasick, is the first to see land (almost), and meets the Taíno kids.

**Recurring characters:**
- *Cristóbal Colón: man in his 40s, long gray-white hair, red cap, dark velvet coat, looking at the horizon with a compass.*
- *Taíno kid: boy about 8, long black hair, bead necklace, red body paint dots, friendly and curious.*
- *Ships: the Santa María (big, slow) and the small caravels Pinta and Niña, white sails with red crosses.*

Start every prompt with the **Nico character block** from `PLAN.md`. Every prompt ends with:
*no subtitles, no on-screen text, no background music, no blood.*

---

### Clip 1: Hook (stowaway)
**Prompt:** Inside a dark wooden barrel on a 15th-century ship, the lid pops open and chubby little Nico
peeks out, holding the camera selfie-style, the deck of the Santa María and big white sails behind him,
sailors walking past, creaking wood and waves. He whispers excitedly in Spanish with a Colombian paisa
accent from Medellín: "¡Quiubo, pues, parceros! Estoy escondido en el barco de Colón, 1492. ¡Ave María!"

### Clip 2: The three ships
**Prompt:** Wide shot from the ship's bow, Nico climbs out of the barrel and points at two smaller sailing
ships alongside on a blue ocean. He says in Spanish with a Colombian paisa accent from Medellín:
"Son tres barcos: la Niña, la Pinta y la Santa María. ¡Y nadie sabe qué hay al otro lado del mar!"

### Clip 3: Seasick
**Prompt:** Big waves, the ship rocks hard. Nico's face turns slightly green, he hugs a wooden mast with
both arms, belly pressed against it, while sailors look tired and grumpy in the background. He says in
Spanish with a Colombian paisa accent from Medellín: "Llevamos más de un mes en el mar… y los marineros ya están bravos. ¡Qué mareo, parce!"

### Clip 4: ¡Tierra!
**Prompt:** Night turning to dawn, moonlight on the sea. A sailor at the top of the Pinta's mast shouts and
points. Nico, in the crow's nest of the Santa María next to a lookout, jumps and points too. He shouts
in Spanish with a Colombian paisa accent from Medellín: "¡Tierra! ¡Tierra! Lo gritó un marinero de la Pinta… ¡pero yo también la vi, pues!"

### Clip 5: Landing (October 12)
**Prompt:** Bright morning, white sand beach with palm trees and turquoise water, small boats landing,
Colón stepping onto the sand holding a flag. Nico jumps off the boat into knee-deep water with a big
splash. He says in Spanish with a Colombian paisa accent from Medellín: "12 de octubre de 1492. Llegamos
a una isla… ¡y aquí ya vivía gente hace muchísimo tiempo!"

### Clip 6: Meeting the Taíno kid
**Prompt:** Under the palm trees, Indigenous Taíno people watch the strangers with curiosity. A Taíno boy
the same age walks up to Nico, they look at each other, then the boy offers him a piece of fruit and
Nico offers a cookie from his hoodie pocket. Nico says to the camera in Spanish with a Colombian paisa
accent from Medellín: "Colón creía que había llegado a la India. ¡Por eso les dijo 'indios'! Se equivocó de continente, pues."

### Clip 7: Present day + the answer to the hook
**Prompt:** Present-day Medellín, a sunny viewpoint with the city and green mountains behind, the
Metrocable gondolas passing. Nico holds a world map and points at Colombia, missing-tooth grin. He says
in Spanish with a Colombian paisa accent from Medellín: "¿Y saben qué? Colombia se llama así por Colón.
¿Cierto que no sabían? ¡Chao pues, parceros!"

### Fact notes
- Columbus left Palos de la Frontera (Spain) on **Aug 3, 1492** with the Niña, the Pinta and the Santa María.
- The voyage took over two months, with a near-mutiny among the crew. On **Oct 12, 1492**, Rodrigo de
  Triana, a sailor on the Pinta, shouted "¡Tierra!".
- They landed on an island the locals called **Guanahaní** (in the Bahamas), home of the Taíno/Lucayan people.
- Columbus believed he had reached Asia ("las Indias"), which is why he called the inhabitants "indios".
- "Colombia" honors Columbus. Francisco de Miranda used the name, and in 1819 it became the name of
  Gran Colombia.
- Nico seeing land and the cookie swap are fiction.
